==============================================================================================================
2x2 family/refit cross-asset copula experiment, six-asset SPY cross-section
==============================================================================================================

Setup:
  - Universe: SPY, NVDA, JNJ, JPM, AAPL, QQQ
  - Per-asset CHMM-N marginals at K = 3 (paper headline K*), fitted once on IS, held fixed in ALL FOUR arms.
  - Arms: {static, rolling-quarterly-refit} x {Gaussian, Student-t} copulas.
  - Quarter partition: 10 OoS blocks of 63 days; the final 5-day partial block is scored and
    reported but EXCLUDED from paired inference (rank-deficient 5-observation correlation target).
  - ROLLING arms: copula refit each quarter on the trailing 252 days; scored on the SAME quarter target.
  - Paths per simulation: 200.  STRICT common random numbers across all four arms via the
    four-argument simulate(model, T, n_paths, crn_seed) interface: identical base normals and
    identical per-asset marginal draws per quarter; the Student-t chi-square mixing stream is
    separate, so it does not shift the draws the arms share.
  - Seed root: 20260422

Notes:
  - All four arms score the identical per-quarter targets with identical marginals, horizons,
    base normals, and marginal draws (strict CRN), so paired differences isolate the design
    factor (refit or family) and quarter-to-quarter target difficulty cancels in d_q.
  - The full-window anchors keep the legacy three-argument simulate + global-seed convention
    so they reconcile with the main-text table; they enter no cross-family contrast.
  - The CHMM-N marginals are not refit in any arm; only the dependence layer moves.
  - The refit and family effects are measured on the SAME quarter-level estimand, so their
    magnitudes are directly comparable (this was not true of the earlier full-window family
    comparison vs quarter-level refit comparison).
  - The rolling t refit can move ν* across the discrete grid {2,3,4,5,6,8,10,15,20,30} per quarter.
