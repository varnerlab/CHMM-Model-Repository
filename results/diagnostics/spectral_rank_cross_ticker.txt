Reading:
  - The K = 18 share tables describe how concentrated the fitted budget is when 17
    modes are available; a median dominant share below 0.90 means the fitted K = 18
    chains spread their ACF budget over several modes at the typical ticker, so the
    K = 18 cross-section alone does NOT establish that a two-mode (K = 3) budget is
    sufficient.
  - The binding-ness question is settled by the ACF-fit block: if the median
    Δ(K3 - K18) near-band MAE is small relative to the zero-curve margin, the extra
    K = 18 modes buy little ACF fit at the typical ticker and the mode budget is not
    the binding constraint at K = 3; a materially positive Δ says it is binding.
  - This is an IS model-vs-sample comparison across the fitted panel; it is not an
    out-of-sample forecast comparison.
